A probability distribution is a model. It is only useful if you know what the variable represents, what you expect from the model and which assumptions you accept by using it.

01 · Start from the phenomenon

What does your variable represent?

Does your variable count something (successes, events, trials), or does it measure a quantity that can take any value in an interval (a duration, a voltage, a pressure)? In the first case the distribution is discrete; in the second, it is continuous.

Your situationA distribution to explore

Suggestions, not an automatic choice: on a small sample, several distributions can produce very similar histograms.

02 · Understand the quantities

Mean, dispersion, probability

Mean The average obtained by repeating the experiment a very large number of times (expected value).
Variance The dispersion around the mean, expressed in squared units.
Standard deviation The square root of the variance: same unit as the measurements.

For a discrete distribution, the height of a bar is a probability . For a continuous distribution, the height is a density: the probability of an interval is the area under the curve, and a density can exceed 1. Try it:

The cumulative distribution function increases from 0 to 1. The 95 % quantile is the value below which at least 95 % of the probability lies.

03 · Experiment

Know the model, observe a sample

The Simulation tab draws pseudo-random values with your parameters, then re-estimates them from the sample. The gap between “true” and “estimated” changes with every draw and narrows as n increases, at the rate . See for yourself:

The seed field makes a draw reproducible: the same seed with the same parameters gives the same sample, which is handy when preparing a teaching example.

More draws are not more measurements.

Generating 10,000 values from a distribution fitted on 20 observations creates no experimental information. The uncertainty on the estimated parameters is not carried over into the simulation.

04 · Keep the file meaningful

One column, one variable

In the Application tab, type or paste your values, or open a .txt or .csv file. A list can be separated by line breaks, spaces or semicolons; both the decimal point and the decimal comma are accepted.

For a table (columns separated by semicolons or tabs), a menu lets you choose the column to analyze; the header row is detected automatically.

time;deviation_Pa 0;0.12 1;0.15 2;0.11

Here, choose “deviation_Pa”. In a comma-separated CSV, write decimals with a point. Non-numeric cells are ignored and their number is reported: nothing is removed silently.

Your values are sent to the server only to perform the calculation; they are neither recorded nor stored.

05 · Interpret

A fit is a starting point

Within the chosen familyThe estimated parameters are the best ones for this distribution; they do not prove that it is the right distribution.
Look before concludingCompare the histogram with the curve, try another distribution, look at the skewness and the kurtosis.
IndependenceThe methods assume independent and identically distributed observations.

Before fitting, examine the acquisition conditions: sensor drift, a change of settings, outliers, systematic effects. The method of moments is sensitive to extreme values, especially on small samples. Finally, fitting a distribution is not an evaluation of measurement uncertainty in the metrological sense.
